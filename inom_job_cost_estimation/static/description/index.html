Key Highlights

A Cost Sheet, Not a Data-Entry Form

Every estimate opens on a visual Project Cost Summary with a cost-bucket donut, per-bucket bars and live Total Cost, Profit, Discount, Selling Price and Final Price — so it reads like dedicated estimation software rather than a plain Odoo screen.

Four Cost Buckets and Quantity Takeoff

Price a job from material, manpower, equipment and indirect cost, and drive material quantities from a BOQ-style measurement sheet with a deduct flag for openings, instead of typing a number nobody can verify.

Markup, Discount and Margin You Can See

Apply markup on cost, add a header or per-line discount that visibly eats margin, and round the final payable to a configurable step — with a history-based markup suggestion and a benchmark against your own similar jobs.

Budget vs Estimate, Estimated vs Actual

Record the customer's budget and see the variance before you commit, then link the estimate to a project and analytic account to compare estimated cost against what is really posted from timesheets, vendor bills and invoices.

Customer-Safe by Design

The PDF, portal and email carry sell-side figures only. Cost, markup and margin live in a separate access-restricted Internal Cost Sheet, alongside Material Requirement and Manpower Plan reports for the site team.

Approvals, Revisions and an Audit Log

Two amount thresholds give you User, Manager and Director tiers, every approval is written to an audit log with the total as it stood at that moment, and approved estimates lock so the quotation can never drift from what was signed off.

Job Costing Analytics Dashboard

One screen for the whole estimating pipeline — estimate count, pipeline value, won value, win rate, average margin and approvals still waiting, each on a clickable KPI card that drills straight into the underlying estimates. Below it, a stage-by-stage pipeline funnel, status and cost-bucket breakdowns, top customers, top materials, a 12-month value trend, performance by job category and a Why We Lost panel. Rendered entirely server-side, with no JavaScript.

A Construction-Style Cost Sheet

Every estimate opens on a visual Project Cost Summary instead of a plain Odoo form — a cost-bucket donut, per-bucket bars, and a running Total Cost, Profit, Discount, Selling Price, Taxes, Rounding and Final Price. Material lines carry unit cost, cost subtotal and selling price side by side, and markup and margin recalculate live as you type, so you always know what the job earns before you send it.

Manpower Priced by Persons, Hours and Rate

Build the labour side from real crew data. Each line takes a role or description, an optional linked employee, the number of persons, the hours and the rate per hour, and returns a cost subtotal and a selling price of its own — so a four-mason week is priced the way you would actually quote it, not as a lump sum nobody can check.

Equipment as a Full Cost Bucket

Plant and machinery get their own bucket, not a line hidden inside overheads. Each entry takes units, duration and a rate basis — per hour, day, week or month — and the rate is applied accordingly, so an excavator for five days and two mixers for five days both price correctly and roll into the same cost sheet.

Quantity Takeoff Measurement Sheet

Drive any material quantity from a proper BOQ-style measurement sheet instead of typing a number. Enter count, length, area or volume rows as Nos. × Length × Width × Height, and tick Deduct on openings such as doors and windows so they are subtracted from the net. The line quantity stays in sync with the sheet and copies onto every revision.

Reusable Rate Cards

Maintain standard rates once and reuse them everywhere. A rate card holds material rates by product and manpower rates by role, and can be set on the estimate header as a default or overridden line by line — so a rate revision is a one-place edit instead of a hunt through old quotes.

Budget vs Estimate and Estimated vs Actual

Record the customer's budget on the estimate and see the variance in both amount and percent before you commit — green while you are inside it, red the moment you are not. Link the estimate to a project and analytic account and the same tab compares your estimated cost against what is really posted from timesheets, vendor bills and invoices.

Project Documents

Keep drawings, site photos, specifications and client mark-ups on the estimate itself, in a Documents tab kept deliberately separate from chatter attachments. Files carry over onto new revisions automatically, and a smart button shows the count at a glance.

Approval Thresholds with Three Tiers

Set two amount thresholds in Settings. Below the first, anyone can approve; between the two, a Job Costing Manager is required; above the second, a Job Costing Director is required. The block names the exact figures so nobody has to guess — and portal acceptance respects the same limits, so a customer can never approve past your internal ceiling.

One-Click Convert to a Sales Quotation

Approved estimates become a sales quotation with one click, grouped into Materials, Manpower, Equipment and Indirect Cost sections with a matching rounding line, so the quotation total equals the estimate's final price to the cent. A smart button links the quotation back to the estimate it came from, and multi-currency conversion uses the customer's pricelist currency at the estimate date's rate.

Customer Portal Approval

Send the estimate and let the customer review and accept it online, with no back-office login. The portal page shows sell-side figures only, offers a PDF download, and records the signer's name and timestamp on acceptance — while still respecting your internal approval threshold.

Customer-Safe PDF Estimate

The customer-facing PDF shows quantities, unit prices, discount percentages, subtotal, taxes, rounding and total — and nothing about cost, markup or margin. A QR code on the document opens the online estimate, so the customer can review and approve straight from their phone.

Restricted Internal Cost Sheet

A separate, access-restricted report carries the numbers the customer must never see: cost, markup percentage, selling price and margin for every line and every bucket, with total cost, markup, margin amount, margin percentage and final price summarised at the end.

Material Requirement Report

Print the material side of an estimate as a purchasing and site list — product, description, quantity, unit of measure, unit cost and cost per line with a total material cost — ready to hand to procurement or the storekeeper.

Manpower Plan Report

Print the labour and equipment side as a resourcing plan — role, employee, persons, hours, man-hours and cost per line, plus equipment with units, duration and rate basis — so the site team knows exactly what the job was priced to use.

Win / Loss Tracking with Loss Reasons

Rejecting an estimate asks for a mandatory reason drawn from a configurable list — price too high, lost to a competitor, timeline did not suit, scope changed, no budget, no response. Lost work becomes data instead of a shrug, and the dashboard rolls it up into a Why We Lost panel that shows where your quotes are actually going.
